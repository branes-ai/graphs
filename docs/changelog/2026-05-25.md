Five graphs PRs (#251-#255) and two embodied-schemas PRs (#84, #85).

## Fixed

- **Thermal-envelope clamp: avg power can never exceed TDP** (closes #177, #121).
  `EnergyAnalyzer` now computes `average_power_w` against a thermally-bound
  latency `max(burst_latency, energy / TDP)`. TDP is a sustained thermal limit,
  so a short burst can't sustain an average above it; energy stays physical
  (truthful joules) and the effective latency floors. Fixed the 983-1175 W
  artifacts for sub-microsecond KPU ops. Universal across mappers; inert unless
  avg power would breach TDP. (`src/graphs/estimation/energy.py`,
  `validation/model_v4/{cli/compare_hardware.py,harness/runner.py,invariants/kpu.py}`,
  `tests/test_energy_thermal_clamp.py`) [#251]
- **KPU leakage now scales with Vdd** (closes #154). `kpu_power_model` scaled
  every dynamic term by `(V/Vnom)^2` but held leakage flat, overstating it 2-3x
  on low-Vdd DVFS profiles. Leakage now scales by
  `(V/Vnom)^leakage_vdd_exponent` (new ProcessNode field, embodied-schemas #84),
  falling back to flat when absent. (`src/graphs/hardware/kpu_power_model.py`,
  `tests/hardware/test_kpu_sku_generator.py`) [#252]
- **KPU dynamic energy single-sourced from ProcessNode; 2x FLOP double-count
  fixed** (closes #81). The loader fed the node's per-MAC FP32 energy straight
  into `energy_per_flop_fp32`, but `sg.flops` counts 2 FLOPs/MAC -> FP32 billed
  at 5.4 pJ/MAC; and `model.energy_scaling` used hardcoded defaults instead of
  node ratios. Now `energy_per_flop_fp32 = node_fp32_per_MAC / 2` and
  `energy_scaling` derives from the node's `energy_per_op_pj` ratios. Effective
  T64: FP32 2.7, INT8 0.30, BF16 1.4 pJ/MAC. FP32/small GEMMs now respect TDP
  unaided; the clamp is a true backstop.
  (`src/graphs/hardware/models/accelerators/kpu_yaml_loader.py`,
  `tests/hardware/test_kpu_energy_single_source.py`) [#253]

## Added

- **KPU family scaling study** (closes #122). `compare_kpu_scaling.py` sweeps
  matmul/vector_add across T64/T128/T256/T768 in BF16 and reports burst +
  thermally-bound latency, energy/inference, efficiency (inf/J == throughput/W),
  and utilization -- 5-panel figure (.png/.pdf) or data table (.csv/.md/.json)
  plus a textual crossover analysis. Registered T128/T256/T768 in
  `SWEEP_HW_TO_MAPPER`. Finding: T128 first beats T64 only at matmul 2048^3
  (wave-quantization saturation below); utilization climbs 53%->70% T64->T768.
  (`validation/model_v4/cli/compare_kpu_scaling.py`,
  `validation/model_v4/harness/runner.py`,
  `tests/validation_model_v4/test_compare_kpu_scaling.py`) [#254]
- **ARM Neoverse N2 single-core reference mapper** (closes #176). A real
  published-IP ARM reference (vs the synthetic AmpereOne 1-core slice) modeling
  N2's 2x 128-bit SVE2 datapath, BF16, INT8 dot-product, 64 KiB L1D, 1 MiB
  private L2, configurable core count. Registered as `ARM-Neoverse-N2-1core`.
  Validation cross-checks ~1.5x FP32 vs the generic ARM helper.
  (`src/graphs/hardware/models/datacenter/arm_neoverse_n2.py`,
  `src/graphs/hardware/mappers/cpu.py`,
  `src/graphs/hardware/mappers/__init__.py`,
  `validation/hardware/test_arm_neoverse_n2.py`) [#255]

Mission dossier · kpu_t128_n7 on tsmc_n7

Edge AI device: Multi-stream tracking & anomaly

Four 1080p streams at 15 Hz, small detector plus re-identification. 2 stages, 5 W, 200 ms. What has to be true of the CPU and the accelerator for that to close.

4 CPU cores and 1 KPU tile. Nothing binds hard: the tightest of memory, compute and power is the CPU at 79%. The part to argue about is the CPU, at 4.5% of its peak on the work it was given.

1. The use case

A fanless, mains- or PoE-powered appliance watching four camera streams: a loading bay, a retail floor, a perimeter. It detects, tracks and flags anomalies on every frame, unattended, for years.

4 x 1920x1080 streams at 15 Hz. 2 stages over 2 tiers, 1 of them on the sense-to-act chain that the 200 ms deadline applies to.

3. The workload

2 stages, from the sensor front end through state estimation, mapping and semantics to reactive control and actuation. They do not run at one rate: from 60 /s to 124 M/s in their own units, which is why each box carries what it demands of an engine rather than a share of some common frame.

The arithmetic is concentrated. det alone is 99% of the operations. The bytes are concentrated somewhere else: det is 89% of the traffic. Those two facts pull the design in different directions, and section 6 is about which one wins.

5. The configuration

One KPU fabric, a cluster of Andes RISC-V cores, a shared fabric and one LPDDR5 interface at tsmc_n7. The capacities below — how many cores and tiles exist — come from the selected design and are an input. What section 6 produces is how many the mission needs, which is the number to compare them against.

The CPU block carries baseline figures: we have measured an Arm Cortex-A78AE, not an Andes core. They stand in for the socket until you replace them, which is what section 6 asks for. Nothing here is a claim about Andes silicon.

Three numbers per compute block, because any two mislead: X, the throughput one server actually delivers; U, the share of wall clock it is busy; E, the ops-weighted mean of the per-class efficiencies it runs at. X and U compose exactly — demand = X × servers × U — while E says how well the kernels suit the machine. Where a stage needs many servers at a low ceiling and another needs few at a high one, E and X/peak diverge, and E is the one that describes the kernels rather than the sizing.

The KPU delivers 1.3 TOP/s per tile at E = 97.5%; the CPU 1.58 GOP/s per core at E = 4.5%.

  • the CPU: 79% of what is there.
  • the KPU: 48% of what is there.
  • datapath power (arithmetic only): 2% of what is there.
  • memory bandwidth: 2% of what is there.

Memory is worth spelling out. The interface carries 4 GB/s of compulsory traffic against 204.8 GB/s of peak, and det is 89% of it on its own — at 60 Hz, a 65.5 MB working set pulled through DRAM on every call.

4. The CPU ask

The CPU needs 3.16 cores and the design has 4.

  • mono (pixel_fixed_function): 3.16 cores at 4.48% of FP32 peak.

Grouped by kernel class, the answer is blunter still: pixel_fixed_function is 3.16 of the 3.16 cores (100%) at 4.48% of peak. So the question for an Andes core is not a general one:

What does it take to run 4.98 GOP/s of pixel_fixed_function in FP32 at materially better than 4.48% of peak?

The leverage is enormous because the baseline is so low. At 10% of peak instead of 4.48%, that class falls from 3.16 cores to 1.41, and the whole CPU requirement from 3.16 to 1.41 — 2 cores provisioned instead of 4.

5. What the accelerator is worth

det has no measured INT8 figure on a CPU, but its INT8 class may run in any wider format and FP32 is measured. At E = 88.8% one core delivers 31.3 GOP/s, so it would need 20 CPU cores and 869 mW — against 0.476 of a tile and 106 mW. That is 42 cores of work per tile at 8.2x less energy.

It is equally an argument for one tile: the catalogued part has 128 tiles, 269x more fabric than this mission can use.

7. The floorplan: where the area goes

An area here is arithmetic, not a drawing. Every block declares its silicon one line at a time: a transistor count and the circuit class it is built in. The node states a density for that class — a named library, a figure in Mtx/mm², and a source — and the area is the division. The largest logic line on this die is kpu's pe_int8: it is 547 Mtx of balanced_logic, and tsmc_n7 puts N7 7.5T HD at 91 Mtx/mm², so it takes 6.009 mm². The largest SRAM line, kpu's l3_sram, is 1,704 Mtx of sram_hd at 280 Mtx/mm², which is 6.085 mm² — and, at 0.052 Mtx per KiB, 32 MiB of storage.

The blocks then roll up the same way on every design. 24.90 mm² of blocks, plus 15% for placement and routing whitespace, is 28.64 mm² of core; a square core is 5.35 mm a side, and a 0.3 mm pad ring on each side makes the die 5.95 mm a side and 35.4 mm². The whitespace fraction and the ring depth are the design's layout record rather than any block's silicon — The reference design's layout overheads, kept equal across the state space so area differences come from the blocks.

Every area on this page is a floor. 10 silicon lines have no figure. 5 blocks — isp, codec, memory, io and fabric — have no anchored line at all, so they occupy nothing in the drawing above; Andes RISC-V, system_cache and safety_island are anchored in part, with core_logic, slc_control and lockstep_r52_cores missing. Nothing is filled in to close them.

What this says about cost, and what it does not

Area is the proxy, and on this page it is the only one. Nothing here carries a wafer price, a defect density or a yield model, so no figure converts mm² into money — and a die area ratio is not a cost ratio, because yield falls with area and a die twice the size costs more than twice as much. What the area does support is the comparison a wafer price would not change the sign of: two configurations at tsmc_n7, priced the same way.

The floor also leans one way. The blocks nothing prices — isp, codec, memory, io and fabric — are exactly the ones that do not shrink when the fabric does. They are fixed area, so the smaller the fabric, the larger the share of the die this page is missing. Missing blocks only ever add area, so at these densities every die here is smaller than the design it describes, and the fixed term is the part that is understated.

What this model cannot tell you

Everything below is required for a safety case and absent here. None of it is a gap we can close by computing harder; each needs evidence this repository does not hold.

  • Worst-case execution time. Every figure on this page is a mean rate from measured throughput. A deadline is met or missed in the tail, and we model no tail: no cache behaviour, no contention, no interrupt latency, no scheduler.
  • Freedom from interference. The chain shares one memory interface with everything else, and the largest other consumer is det at 3.93 GB/s — 2% of the interface. Nothing here bounds what that contention does to a period on the chain.
  • The safety island cannot be sized. The design carries one (island_sram anchored, lockstep_r52_cores not), but the catalogue states no compute capability for it, so no stage can be placed on it and this page cannot tell you what could run there instead.
  • Integrity level, fault coverage, diagnostic coverage, FIT rates and safe-state transition time. The catalogue carries none of these, so this page makes no claim about any standard, and its numbers cannot be cited towards one.

Read the table as a necessary condition, not a sufficient one, and read it against the efficiencies in it. A loop that does not close here does not close at these efficiencies — a faster implementation of the same kernel could close it, which is exactly what section 6 asks for. A loop that closes here has passed only the easiest of the tests it has to pass.

What this rests on

Four kinds of figure, and the page says which each is:

  • Catalogue. The mission, its rates, per-call operation and byte counts, the SoC composition, the node's energy per operation.
  • Measured. CPU efficiencies from orin_nano_measured_v1, with the run behind each figure cited in the table. These are measured on Arm Cortex-A78AE cores in a Jetson Orin Nano — our reference baseline, not a claim about any other core. They are why the CPU requirement is stated as a throughput target rather than a core count.
  • Ceiling. KPU efficiencies are our domain-flow upper bound, taken from the smallest core with a stated schedule (kpu_t64_core). A ceiling bounds, it does not predict, so every tile count is a lower bound.
  • Gap. Named, never filled.

Area. Section 7 takes the die apart: transistors from the IP templates, densities from the node's libraries, and a least-squares line over the catalogued family for how the fabric scales (0.1665 mm² a tile plus 0.287 mm²). One column there is open. We anchor the CPU cluster's caches but not its core logic, because no absolute core area is published for the baseline. An Andes core area would close it outright.
